Notes on Mediations On Moloch by Scott Alexander

2026-10-05

These are notes - not a comprehensive summary or review of the essay - they do not suffice as substitute for reading the original.

Raw notes

PART I

There are systems people participate in, where the participants who perpetuate the system don't like it, but they keep upholding the system because stopping would be too costly, and coordinating is not possible.

Once one agent learns how to become more competitive by sacrificing a common value, all its competitors must also sacrifice that value or be outcompeted and replaced by the less scrupulous. Therefore, the system is likely to end up with everyone once again equally competitive, but the sacrificed value is gone forever. From a god’s-eye-view, the competitors know they will all be worse off if they defect, but from within the system, given insufficient coordination it’s impossible to avoid.
Not all such multi-agent traps are a race to the bottom - some are stuck at a local minima.

PART II

Scott saw Moloch in Las Vegas, when humans directed their brilliance and energy to build massive works that exploit neurobiology, economics, and regulatory regimes, rather than doing what is best for humanity. (gambling = negative risk-benefit ratios get an emotional valence associated with slightly positive risk-benefit ratios)

PART III

Why are we not 'heading down-hill', so to speak? Why aren't we racing towards subsistance levels. Three bad reasons:

  1. We're in a temporary age of abundance because there is a lot of resources we can yet exploit. We'll reach the point of subsistance eventually.
  2. There are physical limits to how far you can push people. Most people will be more productive if they get to enjoy leisure.
  3. Incentives are sometimes alligned. Capitalism allows making profit and caring for peoples' values to allign. (this is fragile. incentives can easily become unalligned, and it's easy to sacrifice values at that point.)
One good reason:
  1. Coordination within a system, e.g. a government over a country, curbs a race to the bottom. Monarchy can ensure this. There is a tradeoff between authoritarianism vs. liberatarianism - tyrany vs. discoordination, coordination vs. multi-polar trap.

PART IV

Multipolar traps are currently restrained by physical limitations, excess resources, utility maximization, and coordination. Let's see how these fold.

  1. Physical limitations are overcome with technological development - replace humans with robots, and don't care for the humans.
  2. Excess resources become a casualty of technological progress - they get utilized/saturated by the new technology quickly.
  3. Utility maximization is threatened when there is a robot that does the work faster and cheaper than humans - utility for the buyer will be maximized by the chaper, better product.
Memes are:
optimizing for a proxy of making us happy, but that proxy can easily get uncoupled from the original goal.
The creationism "debate" and global warming "debate" and a host of similar "debates" in today’s society suggest that memes that can propagate independent of their truth value has a pretty strong influence on the political process.
as technology (which I take to include knowledge of psychology, sociology, public relations, etc) tends to infinity, the power of truthiness relative to truth increases, and things don’t look great for real grassroots democracy.
coordination is at risk because memes might evolve that don't have our actual wellbeing as a selection mechanism. Funny quote when taken out of context:
even though they are outbreeding us, we are outmeme-ing them, and that gives us a decisive advantage
Asmptotically, Moloch might destroy all consciouss life - feed everything into the Machine, not just 99,999999999%.

PART V

This section says that we don't defeat Moloch - races to be bottom - by brining them about, hoping that we migth be spared or rewarded in some way. You will end up worse off - don't realize Moloch, it won't save you. Just because something can/will win, does not make it's victory good.

PART VI

There is a tension between what is evolutionary fit, and what is best for humans. Patriachy might be more evolutionary fit, but it bumps against women's desire to do more than just raise children.

Wrang Franklin argues that we should have Power Over Nature, that we should be the ones to shape the world 'in our image', and not be subjects of Nature's Laws.
Alexander's catch: You will be outcompeted by those who do not follow your rules. If your society ensured that it does not fall victim to Capitalism, Evolution, War, and Memetics, it will fall to a 'society' that does.
But again, submitting to these Forces of Nature does not save you (PART V).

PART VII

Our only option is to seek Power Over Nature - it has the least hubris. Believing that you have to subdue Nature takes less hubris than believing that Nature cares about your, your values, your walled garden, or is willing to negotiate with you so that you can live at its expense.

PART VIII

We have a God on our side - the God of our values. We have to stand for our God so that we may defeat Moloch. We either serve our God, or we serve Moloch. We either preseve our values, or we sacrifice them to the god who will help us win the next battle. In the long run, there is only one way to Win.
