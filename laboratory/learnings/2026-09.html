Linux

Q: How are file descriptors used when you open a file in C or Python?

A: A C or Python program will execute as a process. This process will have a process control block (PCB) which will have three file descriptors (0: stdin, 1: stdout, 2: stderr) by default. If your program opens a file, a new file descriptor (FD) will be created, so that you can read from or write to that file. The FD will have an ID, e.g. 3, which your program can then use to read or write data, e.g. "read 8 bytes from the file." The OS has a global "Open File Description Table", which stores information like a file's inode, current access/index/offset point, read/write permission, and other information. The inode stores the actual address on disk.

South African History

I learnt a lot about South African history this month. I learnt that the Khoi and San people were the first people to live in SA, they have lived here for thousands of years. Around 300 CE, Bantu speaking people arrived in the north eastern side of SA, and integrated and displaced the Khoi or San. Around 1000 CE - 1300 CE, there was a relatively powerful pre-colonial empire in SA called the Mapungupwe empire. Jan van Ribiek crashed in the Cape ~1647, which lead to him arriving in SA in 1652 to setup a rest station for the Dutch in the Cape. The Franchise and Ballot act was passed in 1892. It increased the requirements of enfranchisement which meant that many black people living in the Cape Colony were disenfranchised. It was driven by Cecil John Rhodes after he saw that greater political engagement by the black populus would lead to him losing power. The Natives Land act was passed in 1913, which made it illegal for land to be sold between races, and black poeple were left with only 13% of the land in South Africa. Hendrik Johanness van der Bijl was an incredibly important scientist/engineer who played a massive role in the industrialization of SA in the 20th century. A Hank Reardan like character - it's as if Elon Musk was born ~50 years earlier and came back to SA at some point. The National Party was elected in 1948, and then Apartheid started. In 1955, several political groups wrote the 'Freedom Charter' which articulated an antithesis to the Apartheid system which SA could strive towards, and many of its ideas, like equal representation, and more equal ownership of South African land, were later incorporated in the current SA constitution which was signed into law in 1996. Steve Biko was the founder of the 'Black Consciousness Movement' which said that black people had been so oppressed and tormented that they did not view themselves with high enough regard. He said that black people had to become aware of their own self worth, that they did not have to wait for the Apartheid government to treat them with dignity - they could find all the approval and respect they needed from within themselves - and Biko thought this to be a psychological hurdle many black people had to overcome. His ideas were influenced by W. E. B Du Bois. I also learnt that many institutions actually improved after Apartheid ended - SARS would be one example. The ANC were also very responsible when it came to national debt :) My respect for the South African constitution grew a lot, and it's incredible when you appreciate all that did not go wrong in the period of ~1990-2005. I learnt a lot by listening to Mbuyiseni Ndlozi's podcast, and his appearance on/collaboration with the SMWX podcast - it's a goldmine of South African history and politics that influence the country today. Although I don't aggree with everything he says, Mbuyiseni has a beautiful mind.
