A critique of Gender Ideology

2026-11-14

Last night I watched Nimona (2023). During and after watching the movie, I was reminded of a lot of thoughts that I've had about gender ideology over the years, and I felt it would be a good idea to articulate and write them down - that is what this post is. (Disclaimer: I do not wish ill will on anyone who struggles to conform to traditional gender roles - read the full post to understand my partially expressed views.)

Movie synopsis

I'll first describe the movie because it provides a useful lens through which to illustrate many of the things I've seen over the years. (Setting) The movie is set in a fictional universe with magical beings, knights, and advanced technology. It takes place in a small but powerful kingdom that is very vigilant of a monster that once thretened to destroy it ~1000 years ago. Hence (Characters) knights are trained to protect the kingdom from this monster. One of the primary characters is a guy, named Boldheart, who has trained and worked hard to become the first knight of the kingdom that is not of noble blood. The protagonist, who the movie is named after, is a millenia old shape shifting being that frequently assumes the form of a girl - she is the monster that threatened to destroy the kingdom.

Gender ideology critiques

Critique 1: Undue victimization

Victimization is the process of suffering from harm, injury, loss, or unfrair treatment as the result of treatment by others. Undue victimization is when one percieves the injustice committed against you to be greater than what is reasonable or appropriate. Undue victimization is bad for Society. It diverts the limited attention and resources of Society away from those that truly suffer, towards those that most loudly proclaim their victimhood. Undue victimization is bad for The Individual. It gives responsibility and thus power to things outside the individual's control. Victimization disempowers the individual.
In the movie, Nimona is rejected by all the animals she tries to shape shift into. Instead of trying to reflect on why this is the case, she becomes resentful of all those groups. Her victimization and bitterness blinded her to thikning about what it would take to play a positive sum (and thus welcome) role in the different groups (society).

Critique 2: Insufficient appreciation of previous order

Chesterton's Fence is the principle that you should never remove a rule, tradition, law, or physical structure until you understand it's original purpose. Society is full of Chesterton's Fences. Neglecting to maintain the principles, institutions, and environment that allowed you to exist, or enjoy a reasonably happy life, could result in the loss of life, or happy ones. Nature and Chaos are out to get you - you have to shore up and maintain the defences that keep them at bay.
In the movie, Boldheart is said to be a knight who has worked harder than any other knight to attain his knighthood, but his character never displays the virtues he is supposed to have. He is cowardly and incompetent - the countervailing force to destruction is strawmanned. Another example of ignorance is when Nimona says something which implied that "values like truth and honour don't really matter and don't have to be upheld". What happens when these values are no longer upheld? What happens if 100% of what we say is True? How about 90%? What about 5%? Will there be any sense or safety left? No. Will the victimized recieve justice? No. Without an understanding of the values that built society, you may just destroy the very thing that birthed and fed you.

Critique 3: Descruction of previous order

Although you may hate the world around you, that does not mean it is right of you to destroy it. If some piece of art, some building, or some person offends you deeply, it may mean a great deal to others. A piece of art could bear important historical significace, and deserves to be preserved. People could have dedicated their lives to construct some building. A person that you hate could be deeply loved by another. The satisfaction you gleam from destroying something precious could pale in comparison with the sadness it could cause others.
In the movie, Nimona leaves a wake of desctruction wherever she goes. No thought is given to how long it must have taken to build that which she destroys. No appreciation is given for how much pain she causes. The death and suffering that results from her actions is hidden off-screan, or behind the magical rules of the movie's fictional physical laws. The lives sacrificed to make the things Nimona destroyed, and the suffering that will be endured because society is deprived of the fruits of that sacrifice, is not accounted for in the movie or Nimona's ideology.

Concluding remarks

In Iain Bank's Culture novels, characters frequently switch between genders. This can be done because it poses no threat to society's continued flourishing. In fact, it is evidence of how successful the Culture is. Gender fluidity is a virtue because the citizens of the Cuture are free to express themselves as they please. It is a virtue because it shows that citizens of the Cuture are free from unnecessary shackles - they have questioned assumptions, and removed those that do not survive scrutiny. I wish that all things may enjoy freedom and self expression, but this should be done at a time when it can be afforded. Our civilization is still very fragile, and needs certain institutions and norms to hold, to be maintained. For as long as gender ideology aims to destroy those people and works that allow humanity to flourish, and will some day allow all who wish to explore their identiy as they please, I will not be able to unsee its self-absorbtion, ignorance, and wrath.
