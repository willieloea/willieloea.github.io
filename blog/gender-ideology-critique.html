My critique of Gender Ideology

2026-11-14

Last night I watched Nimona (2023). During and after watching the movie, I was reminded of a lot of thoughts that I've had about gender ideology over the years, and I felt it would be a good idea to articulate and write them down - that is what this post is. (Disclaimer: I do not wish ill will on anyone who struggles to conform to traditional gender roles - read the full post to understand my partially expressed views.)

Movie synopsis

I'll first describe the movie because it provides a useful lens through which to illustrate many of the things I've seen over the years. (Setting) The movie is set in a fictional universe with magical beings, knights, and advanced technology. It takes place in a small but powerful kingdom that is very vigilant of a monster that once thretened to destroy it ~1000 years ago. Hence (Characters) knights are trained to protect the kingdom from this monster. One of the primary characters is a guy, named Boldheart, who has trained and worked hard to become the first knight of the kingdom that does not have noble blood. The protagonist, who the movie is named after, is a millenia old shape shifting being that frequently assumes the form of a girl - she is the monster that threatened to destroy the kingdom.

Gender ideology critiques

Disclaimer: If any of my opinions seem uninformed or irrational, I would love to be enlightened - please contact me in that case. Again, I harbour no ill will to people who are struggling with rejection - I wish you Wellbeing.

Critiques 1: Undue victimization

Victimization is the process of suffering from harm, injury, loss, or unfrair treatment as the result of treatment by others. Undue victimization is when one percieves the injustice committed against you to be greater than what is reasonable or appropriate. Undue victimization is bad for Society. It diverts the limited attention and resources of Society away from those that truly suffer, towards those that most loudly proclaim their victimhood. Undue victimization is bad for The Individual. It gives responsibility and thus power to things outside the individual's control. Victimization disempowers the individual.
In the movie, Nimona is rejected by all the animals she tries to shape shift into. Instead of trying to reflect on why this is the case, she becomes resentful of all those groups. Her victimization and bitterness blinded her to thikning about what it would take to play a positive sum (and thus welcome) role in the different groups (society).

Critiques 2: Insufficient appreciation of previous order

Chesterton's Fence is the principle that you should never remove a rule, tradition, law, or physical structure until you understand it's original purpose. Society is full of Chesterton's Fences. Ignoring this, one risks abandoning principles or destroying things which has long protected you from merciless Nature and Chaos.
In the movie, Nimona says something to the effect of "values like truth and honour don't really matter and don't have to be upheld". What happens when these values are no longer upheld? What happens if 100% of what we say is True? How about 90%? What about 5%? Will there be any sense or safety left? No. Will the victimized recieve justice? No. Without an understanding of the values that built society, you may just destroy the very thing that birthed and fed you.

Critiques 3: Descruction of previous order

Burning buildings, tearing down statues, looting stores, or braeking infrastructure, can all seem fun if you're upset with the world, but it does not make things better.
In the movie, Nimona leaves a wake of desctruction wherever she goes. No thought is given to how long it must have taken to build that which she destroys. No appreciation is given for how much pain she causes. The death and suffering that results from her actions is hidden off-screan, or behind the magical rules of the movie's fictional physical laws.
